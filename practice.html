Which amino acid's side chain is most useful for buffering near physiological pH (7.4)?

Histidine

Histidine's imidazole side chain has a pKa of about 6.0, closer to 7.4 than any other side chain, so it's meaningfully both protonated and deprotonated near physiological pH.

Tris shifts about -0.028 pH units per degree C. If you set a Tris buffer to pH 8.00 at 25 C, what is it in a 4 C cold room?

About 8.6

Cooling by 21 degrees raises the pH by roughly 21 x 0.028, which is about 0.59 units. This is why Tris buffers should be adjusted at the temperature where they will be used.

A researcher assays an enzyme with and without a new compound. Vmax is unchanged, but the apparent Km rises fourfold. The compound is most likely a:

Competitive inhibitor

Only one mechanism can leave the ceiling in place: if saturating substrate still reaches the same maximum rate, the inhibitor must be losing the race for the active site at high [S]. Anything that touches the enzyme-substrate complex drags Vmax down with it.

For a strong acid, [H⁺] equals:

The starting concentration

Strong acids fully dissociate, so [H⁺] equals whatever concentration of acid you started with, no equilibrium calculation needed.

A solution at pH 3 has how many times more [H⁺] than one at pH 5?

100×

pH is a base-10 log scale, so each whole unit is a tenfold change in [H⁺]. Two units apart is 10², not 2, which is the mistake of reading the scale as linear.

A student weighs 6.2 g of product where stoichiometry predicts 8.0 g, then discovers the balance has been reading 0.3 g high all day. What is the corrected percent yield?

73.8%

The offset is on the measured mass, so subtract it from the recovered amount before dividing. Correcting the theoretical value instead, or adding rather than subtracting, both move the answer up when it should come down.

Crossing Aa × Aa (one gene, complete dominance) gives what phenotype ratio?

3:1

3 out of 4 offspring show the dominant phenotype (AA, Aa, Aa), 1 shows recessive (aa). The 1:2:1 trap is the genotype ratio, not the phenotype ratio.

Under Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium with q = 0.3, what fraction of the population is heterozygous?

0.42

Heterozygote frequency is the 2pq term. The 0.09 trap is q squared, the recessive homozygotes, and 0.49 is p squared.

A pediatrician delivers a type O newborn. The mother is type A and the listed father is type AB, and he disputes paternity. What does the blood typing establish?

AB passes A or B, so this man is excluded

An AB individual has no O allele to give, so every child of his carries A or B and shows it. Exclusion is the only thing ABO typing can do well: it never confirms paternity, because millions of men share any blood type.

Textbooks give 30, 32, 36 or 38 ATP for one glucose's total aerobic yield. That disagreement comes from two different values used for:

The P/O ratio per NADH

Older courses use 3 ATP per NADH and 2 per FADH2, which lands on 36 or 38. Modern measured values of 2.5 and 1.5 land on 30 or 32, and the shuttle then picks between each pair.

Why is the urea cycle usually costed at 4 ATP-equivalents rather than 3?

Step 3 goes ATP to AMP

Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I spends 2 ATP outright. Argininosuccinate synthetase spends only one ATP but breaks two high-energy bonds doing it, so it is counted as 2 rather than 1.

A muscle cell switches to anaerobic conditions mid-contraction. Per glucose, what is its ATP yield now?

2 ATP

Only glycolysis keeps paying, and it pays net, not gross: 4 made minus 2 invested. The cycle's 2 GTP go too, because the cycle stalls once NAD+ stops coming back from the chain.

What is the net ATP yield of glycolysis per glucose molecule?

2

4 ATP are produced in the payoff phase, but 2 were invested early on, net 2 ATP, plus 2 NADH and 2 pyruvate.

How many codons specify amino acids, out of 64 total?

61

Three of the 64 are stop codons, so they come off the total. Answering 20 confuses the number of amino acids with the number of codons that encode them.

An RNA prep reads 260/280 of 2.05 and 260/230 of 1.2. It looks clean by one measure and not the other. What is the most likely contaminant, and what does it do downstream?

Guanidine or phenol, which inhibits the RT step

The two ratios watch different windows, so a prep can pass one and fail the other. Protein shows up at 280 nm, not 230, and a ratio that passes one check never clears the prep on its own.

A solution is made hyperosmotic to plasma by adding urea. Is it hypertonic?

No, urea crosses the membrane freely

Tonicity counts only the solutes a membrane blocks, so a permeant one raises the osmometer reading without holding water on either side. Treating osmolarity and tonicity as the same number is what makes this look hypertonic.

Cardiac output is 5.4 L/min and heart rate is 72 bpm. What is the stroke volume?

75 mL per beat

Rearranging the cardiac output formula puts output over rate, and the litres have to become millilitres before the division or the answer lands a thousand times too small. Multiplying the two instead gives a volume no ventricle holds.

A climber at altitude has raised 2,3-BPG and a respiratory alkalosis from hyperventilating. These two push the oxygen curve in opposite directions. What is the effect at the tissues?

The 2,3-BPG right shift wins, unloading is preserved

A right shift is lower affinity and a higher P50, and this page lists 2,3-BPG as a right-shifter separate from the Bohr effect proper. Loading happens at the lung's high pressure and unloading at the tissue's low one, so a shift can help at one end without undoing the other.

What is the approximate molar mass of NaCl?

58.4 g/mol

Sodium contributes about 23 and chlorine about 35.5, and the two atomic masses simply add. Picking 40 confuses the atomic mass of calcium for the whole salt.

How many moles are in 500 mL of a 2 M solution?

1 mol

Molarity counts moles per litre, so the volume has to be in litres before multiplying. Reading 500 mL as 500 L, or forgetting to convert at all, is what produces the other answers here.

A protocol calls for 1:5000 antibody in 200 µL, but your smallest reliable pipette volume is 1 µL. What does this site's calculator have you do?

Two steps, 1:100 then 1:50, via 100 µL of 1:100

Dilution factors multiply, so splitting one impossible factor into two possible ones lands on the same final concentration with every volume above the pipette floor. Enlarging the final volume only helps if you then check the floor again, and at 1 mL the antibody is still only 0.2 µL.
