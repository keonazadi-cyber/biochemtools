Arginine titration curve

Arginine has 3 ionizable groups, with pKa values of 2.17, 9.04 and 12.48. Its isoelectric point is pI 10.76, and the curve has 2 equivalence points and 3 buffering plateaus.

The ionizable groups

GrouppKaBehaviour
alpha-carboxyl2.17loses H+ as pH rises
alpha-amino9.04keeps H+ until pH passes it
guanidinium side chain12.48keeps H+ until pH passes it

Where it buffers

A weak acid buffers within about one pH unit either side of its pKa, which is the flat part of the curve. For arginine that means:

Buffering rangeCentred on
pH 1.17 to 3.17pKa 2.17
pH 8.04 to 10.04pKa 9.04
pH 11.48 to 13.48pKa 12.48

Equivalence points

Halfway between two pKa values the previous group is fully titrated and the next has not started. Those are the steep parts.

PointpH
Equivalence point 1pH 5.60
Equivalence point 2pH 10.76

Working out the pI

Arginine has three ionizable groups, so the pI is the average of the two pKa values that bracket the neutral form: (9.04 + 12.48) / 2 = 10.760, published as pI 10.76. The third group is already fully protonated or fully deprotonated there, so it does not enter the average.

Where the numbers come from

pKa values are the Lehninger set, the same ones used across this site. The curve is generated from those values with the Henderson-Hasselbalch relation for each group, and the pI is solved solved numerically as a check on the arithmetic above, which agrees to within 0.03 pH units.
